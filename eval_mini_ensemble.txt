Interpretation
Why Distill (35.43%) > Ensemble (26.53%) — counterintuitive but explainable
Normally on the full dataset, ensemble ≥ distill > fusion. Here the order is inverted:

Fusion branch is weak here: Only the last keyframe's 6 cameras are used for 2D features. ~30% of 3D points have no 2D feature at all (they get assigned "unknown"). When the ensemble blends the 3D distill prediction with a noisy/empty fusion prediction, it degrades what was already a good distill answer.

Pretrained model generalises well: The 3D MinkUNet was trained on the full nuScenes training split. On mini val scenes it produces strong predictions for every voxel — distill doesn't depend on having sparse 2D coverage.

Small sample (10 scenes): Stats are noisy. A few bad ensemble decisions on large classes (bus: 69% → 29%, person: 36% → 6%) dominate the mean enough to pull it well below distill.

Class-level insights
Distill clearly wins on:

Large vehicles (bus 69%, car 72.5%, truck 64%): geometrically distinctive — the 3D model learned their shapes clearly.
Background structures (drivable surface 74.4%, manmade 70.3%, vegetation 74.0%): dense, consistent geometry.
Person 35.8%: Distill picks up pedestrians well; ensemble assignment of fusion features (often missing around small objects) hurts it to 6.1%.
Ensemble wins on:

Trailer (2.5% → 36.2%): Distill confuses trailer with truck/bus. The 2D fusion features (visual appearance: trailer looks visually distinct) correct some of those errors.
Bicycle (0% → 5.1%): Bicycles are rare (~1463 total points across all 10 scenes), so distill predicts 0 correct. The fusion features add a small visual signal that helps.
Universal weak spots (both methods):

other flat (0–0.5%): Parking lots, crosswalks — visually and geometrically ambiguous, confused with drivable surface.
bicycle, motorcycle, traffic cone: Rare classes with few points — both methods struggle with low representation.
sidewalk (9–12%): Easily confused with terrain and drivable surface in urban scenes.
